Home Remodeling in Coral Gables

Remodeling in the City Beautiful.

Coral Gables was drawn by George Merrick in the 1920s, and much of it still looks the part: Mediterranean Revival homes with barrel tile roofs and arched loggias in the Country Club Section and Riviera, coral rock cottages near the Granada Golf Course, and waterfront estates in Cocoplum and Gables Estates. What owners of these homes actually want is rarely a teardown. It is the original architecture wrapped around a modern interior, with open sightlines, real closets, new mechanicals, and impact glass. A serious home remodeling project here keeps the character and quietly replaces everything behind it.

Remodeling here runs through the City of Coral Gables Development Services Department, and anything that touches the exterior, from windows and doors to paint colors, goes to the Board of Architects before the Building Division reviews it. Designated historic homes add a Certificate of Appropriateness through the city's preservation staff or board. Impact windows and doors need Miami-Dade product approvals for the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, and if the project involves site work, the city's tree protections apply. None of this is a reason to panic. It is a reason to hire a contractor who has been through it before.

PGX Builders Group has spent 20-plus years building in South Florida, with more than 150 remodels and additions delivered, every one permitted and inspected. In Coral Gables we work the way the neighborhoods expect: a fixed written scope before demolition starts, a supervisor on site daily, floors and finishes protected, and crews who understand that a guard gate in Cocoplum is not a suggestion. One team carries your project from estimate to final inspection to warranty, so there is exactly one phone number to call when you have a question.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Do I need Board of Architects approval to remodel my Coral Gables home?
Only if the work shows from outside. Interior remodels go through the Building Division without a Board of Architects hearing. The moment your scope touches windows, doors, exterior colors, or the roofline, the Board reviews it first, and its approval is required before the building permit advances. We prepare submittals with that sequence in mind, so the review happens once instead of three times.
My house is a designated historic landmark. Can I still remodel it?
Yes, and plenty of Gables owners have. Exterior changes to a designated property need a Certificate of Appropriateness from the city's historic preservation staff or board before permits are issued, and minor in-kind work can often be approved administratively. Interiors are generally yours to reimagine. The practical effect is a longer runway, not a locked door, and we build that runway into the schedule from day one.
How long does a whole-home remodel take in Coral Gables?
Longer than a bathroom, shorter than you fear, and honestly dependent on approvals. Interior-only scopes can move quickly once permitted. Projects involving Board of Architects review, historic approvals, or structural engineering typically spend a few months in design and permitting before construction, and construction itself commonly runs several months to a year for a full house. We give you a written schedule with the estimate and then supervise daily to defend it.
What does a whole-home remodel cost in Coral Gables?
There is no honest one-number answer, because a cosmetic refresh and a full gut with new mechanicals are different animals. What we can promise is a fixed written scope with real allowances instead of a teaser number that grows through change orders. PGX is a high-end contractor: we are rarely the lowest bid, and our clients hire us so the number they sign is the number they pay, barring genuine surprises we document in writing.
Can we live in the house during a remodel?
Sometimes, and we will tell you plainly when the answer should be no. Phased work can keep a wing livable if the scope allows, with dust barriers, protected pathways, and mechanical continuity. Full-gut projects with open ceilings and no working kitchen are miserable to camp inside, so most Gables clients relocate for the heavy phase and return early for finishes. We plan the sequencing either way before demolition starts.
