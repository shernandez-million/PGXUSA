Kitchen Remodeling in Wilton Manors

Where the wall used to be.

Mid-century builders in Wilton Manors put the kitchen in a corner and shut the door on it. Walk a ranch off NE 26th Street, or a block in Coral Estates, and you find the same plan repeated: a galley barely wide enough for two people, a pass-through to the dining room, and a Florida room next door doing very little. The kitchen remodeling projects we take on here almost always begin by removing that wall, then rebuilding the room around an island four people can sit at.

A kitchen at this level pulls building, electrical, plumbing and mechanical into one submittal, so it clears all four review stops at Wilton Manors Community Development Services and gets inspected at each rough-in. Two things surprise owners of 1950s slab houses. First, the electrical service: a panel sized for a 1958 range and a window unit will not carry induction, a double oven and a wine column. Second, what sits under the slab, because original cast iron has to be cut out, replaced and inspected before anyone pours back. Enlarge the window over the sink and High-Velocity Hurricane Zone product approvals join the set.

Cabinet shops do their sharpest work for builders who field-measure after framing and before ordering, and who template stone from the installed boxes rather than from a drawing. That is how we sequence a kitchen. Cabinetry and appliances with long lead times get ordered up front, so nobody is living around an open room while a truck crosses Georgia. Floors, doors and the route from the driveway get protected every morning, not once at the start. One fixed written scope, one supervisor, one company answering the phone from estimate through warranty.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Does a kitchen remodel here really need a permit?
Anything past paint and a like-for-like appliance swap does. Moving plumbing, adding circuits, changing a hood duct or taking out a wall all get permitted through Community Development Services, with separate trade reviews and inspections. The upside is a record. When you sell, the buyer's inspector finds a permitted kitchen instead of a mystery, and permitting something retroactively always costs more than sequencing it correctly the first time.
Can the kitchen open into the Florida room?
Usually, and in Wilton Manors it is the single most requested change. These are concrete-block houses, so the opening needs an engineer to size a beam and detail how the load travels down to the footing. That drawing goes in with the permit set and is inspected before it is covered. Occasionally a wall carries something awkward, like a plumbing stack or a duct main, and the honest answer is a wide cased opening rather than a full removal.
How long is my kitchen out of service?
Plan on roughly two months of construction for a full kitchen, longer when structure or a service upgrade is in the scope. The clock that matters starts earlier: custom cabinetry and stone are ordered months ahead, and we will not demolish anything until the cabinets have a confirmed delivery date. Most clients rig a hot plate and a fridge out in the carport or Florida room and stay put for the duration.
Does my kitchen have to meet hurricane code?
The cabinetry does not. The openings do. Every exterior opening in Broward is a High-Velocity Hurricane Zone opening, so if the scope enlarges a window, adds a door to the patio or changes a slider, the unit needs an approved product listing and the installation needs sealed engineering. Copies of the product approvals go into the permit package with the plans. We handle that paperwork so the kitchen is not a conversation at your next insurance inspection.
What does a high-end kitchen run here?
Selections drive it far more than square footage. Custom cabinetry, natural stone and a serious appliance package can absorb most of a budget before a single trade shows up, and structural work or a service upgrade sits on top of that. We scope in writing after a walk-through, which means you are pricing a specification instead of a hopeful round number. We are not the low bid, and we would rather say so in the first meeting.
