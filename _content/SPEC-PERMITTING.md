# PGX — Permitting & Compliance pages (addendum to SPEC.md)

Everything in `_content/SPEC.md` applies. This addendum covers what the permitting page for an
area must actually be about, because permitting is the service PGX leads with and the one most
competitors treat as an afterthought.

Permitting is jurisdiction-driven, so the page lives or dies on getting the local authority right.

## What the copy must cover

- **The actual permitting authority for this area**, by name, and how work is filed there.
  Some areas are their own city or village; Fisher Island and Ponce-Davis are unincorporated
  Miami-Dade; the Fort Lauderdale neighbourhoods all file with the City of Fort Lauderdale.
  Broward has a county-level layer (Board of Rules and Appeals) that Miami-Dade does not.
- **What actually needs a permit** in this area's housing stock, and what commonly gets done
  without one — because that is what creates the problem below.
- **Open permits.** The single highest-intent reason someone searches for this. An open or
  expired permit from a prior owner blocks a sale or a refinance, and it surfaces at the worst
  possible moment. Explain what closing one out involves.
- **HVHZ product approvals** — both counties are in the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, so
  windows, doors and roofing need approved products and documentation.
- **Inspections** — phased, and passing first time is the difference between a schedule and a mess.
- Where genuinely relevant to the area: **40/50-year recertification** (Miami-Dade),
  Broward's equivalent, and **milestone inspections / SIRS** for condo-heavy areas.
- **Historic or design review** where it applies to that jurisdiction.

## Angle

This is not a sales page about paperwork. It is about removing risk from a large transaction.
The buyer is either (a) planning work and wants it done legally, or (b) already has a problem —
an open permit, a failed inspection, a recertification notice — and needs it solved.
Write for both, and let the urgency of the second be felt without melodrama.

## Hard rules beyond SPEC.md

1. **Never state a permit fee, a review timeline in days, or a legal deadline as fact.** These
   change and vary by scope. Frame qualitatively ("weeks, not days", "before your closing date").
2. Never imply PGX can shortcut, expedite improperly, or get around a requirement.
3. Do not claim PGX is a licensed permit expediter, engineer or architect unless stating that
   PGX coordinates those professionals — which is accurate.
4. Verify the authority name with WebSearch before asserting it. If unsure, refer to it
   generically ("the city's building department") rather than guessing.
